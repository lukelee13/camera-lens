1. Basic Principles of Infrared Lenses

Visible light occupies only a narrow portion of the electromagnetic spectrum, while infrared radiation has longer wavelengths and carries the thermal radiation information of objects themselves. Ordinary glass is nearly opaque to infrared light, so special materials must be used to manufacture an **IR lens** that allows infrared radiation to pass through and focus onto a detector. The core function of an infrared lens is to collect and focus infrared light to form an image that can be read by a thermal imager or detector. Its design requires precise control of refractive index, dispersion, and thermal expansion to maintain sharp imaging under varying temperature conditions.

2. Detailed Classification of the Four Wavebands
The infrared spectrum is divided into several atmospheric windows according to wavelength, corresponding to different types of **infrared lens**:

- **Near-Infrared (NIR)**: Wavelength approximately 0.7–1.4 μm. The **NIR infrared lens** operates in this band and is commonly used in night vision, fiber-optic communications, and certain industrial inspections. It can capture both reflected light and weak thermal signals.
- **Short-Wave Infrared (SWIR)**: Wavelength approximately 0.9–2.5 μm. The **SWIR lens** and **short wave infrared lens** can penetrate haze and thin smoke, making them suitable for semiconductor inspection, agricultural monitoring, and remote sensing.
- **Mid-Wave Infrared (MWIR)**: Wavelength 3–5 μm. The **MWIR lens** and **mid wave infrared lens** are highly sensitive to high-temperature targets and offer long detection ranges. They are widely used in missile guidance, aerospace thermal imaging, and scientific experiments.
- **Long-Wave Infrared (LWIR)**: Wavelength 8–14 μm. The **LWIR lens** and **long wave infrared lens** specifically capture thermal radiation from objects at ambient temperatures and form the core of the **thermal imaging lens**. They are extensively applied in security, firefighting, power line inspection, and medical temperature measurement.

3. Key Materials and Optical Properties
Common materials include germanium (Ge, high refractive index, ideal for LWIR), zinc selenide (ZnSe, broad-band transmission), silicon (Si, lightweight, suitable for MWIR), and chalcogenide glass (moldable and lower cost). Material selection directly determines the lens transmittance, thermal stability, and price. High-performance **infrared lens** products are usually coated with anti-reflection films to minimize surface reflection losses.

4. Typical Application Scenarios
- Security and surveillance: Using LWIR lens to clearly identify people and vehicles in complete darkness.
- Industrial inspection: Detecting equipment overheating or insulation defects with a **thermal imaging lens**.
- Medical diagnosis: Non-contact body temperature screening and inflammation localization.
- Military and security: Long-range target detection and nighttime operations.
- Scientific research and environmental protection: Forest fire early warning and gas leak detection.

5. Technology Development Trends
Current infrared lenses are evolving toward athermalization, continuous zoom, lightweight design, and intelligence. Athermalized designs maintain focus stability under extreme temperature differences; motorized continuous zoom provides greater operational flexibility; and AI algorithms can analyze thermal image anomalies in real time for automatic alerts. Multi-spectral fusion technology is integrating visible light, near-infrared, and long-wave infrared information to deliver more comprehensive environmental perception.
